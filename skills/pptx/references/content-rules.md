# Slide content, copy and AI-smell guardrails

Adapted from carnot-tech/jinba-consulting-pptx-skill (`references/slide-rules.md`, `references/ai-smell-lexicon.md`, MIT) and miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop (`skills/antislop-copywriting/SKILL.md`, MIT). Load with `references/design-system.md`. Geometry and color still come from there.

## Before building

- Write the storyline first: one-line conclusions (future titles) in an order that reads like one speech; choose slide types after.
- One slide, one message, one theme.
- Verify facts at source: numbers, dates and proper nouns.
- Confirm audience, meeting, decision needed, expected finish level; with no background, ship one argument note or text instead of a long deck.

## Titles

- Titles are conclusions in ordinary sentences, with subject and object intact. Allow two lines instead of cutting words.
- Titles must stand alone; avoid cross-slide references (`this`, `that`, `so far`).
- Do not prefix a label before a colon; put the topic in the kicker.
- No all-caps slogans and no title coloring; emphasis belongs in words.
- Ten slides with the same sentence shape usually means the template was copied, not the argument.
- Break two-line titles at a meaning boundary; do not leave one to three stranded characters.
- Numbers in titles must match slide content.
- Every chart slide carries its takeaway, usually in the title.
- State evidence, not certainty; do not claim more than the supporting table does.
- Use relative terms from a named actor's view (`vendors`, not `external`).
- Keep competitive and factual descriptions neutral; put judgement in the conclusion line.

## No subtitle rows

- No subtitle or lead-in line under the title.
- No label row under a chart or table; state what it is in the title or column header.
- No restated heading under a card, lane or phase label; add mechanism, numbers or names.
- One image caption is allowed only as a step instruction (what to press or where to look).

## Slide structure

- Stacked unrelated blocks are forbidden; columns side by side, vertical stacking only inside one ordered chart.
- One table per slide; put premises left and table right when premises define it.
- Left = evidence or premises, right = commentary; right-column headings name the consequence.
- Align column bottoms; no short right column floating above empty space.
- Two columns with real cause -> consequence get one triangle arrow between them, placed once at vertical centre; omit for plain table + explanation.
- Premises left, examples right, consequences further right.
- As-is / to-be compares side by side, not stacked.
- Supporting right-column evidence lives in the left chart as a matching band, using the same bold term.
- One representative slide before mass expansion of one template.
- Lists of review comments and open items do not ship in the main deck; collect them in an appendix slide.

## Tables and charts

- Use an axis-bearing table over a card grid for comparison.
- Compare on the same axes and order a second view uses.
- Add a direction axis where row order implies one.
- Show every claim as a table on the same value chain when overstatement is a risk.
- Do not use tables for overlap, schedules, processes or assignments; use Venn, Gantt, chevrons or flow.
- Repeated per-column sub-labels belong in the row axis.
- Color one turning-point step differently and isolate it as its own chevron.

## Copy and wording

- Never invent facts, names, numbers, dates, quotes or citations; specificity comes from source or user.
- Remove AI vocabulary first: unlock, elevate, empower, delve, seamless, revolutionary, game-changer, and similar abstract intensifiers.
- Remove significance inflation (`marking a pivotal moment`, `new era`), unnamed authority (`experts say`), signposting, chatbot closers and fake-candid openers.
- Do not force lists into threes, negative-parallel (`not just X, it is Y`) or staccato fragments; use the count the content has.
- Use the actor: prefer `We rewrote the pricing page` over `The pricing page was updated`, and do not give inanimate subjects human verbs.
- Replace em dash asides with period, comma, colon or parentheses; one deliberate em dash can stay.
- Remove filler and excessive hedging; keep one qualifier when needed.
- CTAs state the specific action; claims carry real numbers or none.
- Copy with no voice reads as machine-made too; keep a real voice and prefer specific, hard-to-fabricate detail.

## AI-smell self-check

- Cut redundancy, over-polite explanation, circution and stock phrases first.
- Remove decoration-as-authority: accent stripes, title underlines, footer bars, emoji headings, traffic-light tables.
- Identical geometry on unrelated messages is an AI-deck tell; vary layout with message type.
- End on concrete facts, not generic positive conclusions.
- Keep status colors paired with a word.
- Machine-style markers to scan: undefined jargon, repeated sentence shapes, unnamed attributions, fabricated specifics.

## Final 30-second check

1. Did you cut filler words, connectors, stock openers and standard closers?
2. Did you remove emoji, symbol decoration and excessive bold?
3. Does it read as something you would say aloud?
4. Is it long enough to trim once more?
5. Are all names, numbers, dates and quotes real or clearly labeled placeholders?
